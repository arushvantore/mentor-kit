Hi all,
Today is the ICPC online round. A few things before you log in.
*Stay calm.* Three hours is longer than it feels in the first twenty minutes. If a problem feels hard at first, that's normal. Breathe, give it a fair amount of time, and keep thinking.
*Try every problem.* Pace yourselves so that you look at every problem at some point during the contest. Make sure no problem goes unread by the end. Don't judge a problem by its position in the set, and don't assume a problem is out of reach until you've actually spent time on it.
*Work as a team.* Talk through an idea before anyone starts coding. While one person debugs, another can think ahead. Trust each other.
*Have fun.* This is what you've been training for. Enjoy the moment when an idea finally clicks.
And one request, the same one Prof. Vipin Pavithran, Regional Contest Director of the ICPC Asia West Amritapuri site, has made to every team in India (his letter is attached, please read it): *don't cheat yourself.* No AI, no outside help, no copying. Beyond the risk of disqualification, it takes away the one thing this contest gives you, which is finding out what your own mind can do. A problem you solve yourself after a struggle will teach you more than any accepted verdict you didn't earn.
I've sat in that chair. Whatever the result, the struggle is the part that stays with you.
All the best. Make yourselves proud.

Learning How to Learn - survey findings and tomorrow’s class
Attendance in this course has been dropping, so instead of assuming we knew why, we ran an anonymous survey last week. 81 of you responded, and we have read every response.
The report is attached. We are sharing it with all of you rather than keeping it internal, so you can see what the batch as a whole said. Some of it is critical of how we have run the course, and we have kept that in.
The clearest finding is that most of you understand the ideas but have not been able to put them into practice - 50 of the 81 said they had not used anything from the course so far, including several who attend regularly and rate the sessions highly.
Tomorrow’s class is built around exactly this. We will work on how you actually implement these techniques: what the first attempt looks like, where it usually breaks down, and how to get past that. You will try them on your own subject, in a form you can use while preparing for midterms.
For students who have not been attending, we strongly recommend attending tomorrow’s class. Nothing in it assumes you were present earlier, and implementation is the part regular attenders have struggled with too.
On timing - this was the most common reason given for skipping. Next week we will check whether the session can be offered on two days so you can pick what suits you. We will update you after that.
Three takeaways from the survey

The ideas are landing, but the practice is not. 50 of 81 have not used anything from the course yet.
Students who could connect the course to their own subjects were more than twice as likely to have used something from it - 55% against 24%. Making that connection clearer is our job.
Very few of you think the sessions are the problem. Of the nine reasons offered for skipping, “not engaging” and “not enough value” scored lowest across the batch.
In tomorrow's class, we will focus on implementation of the techniques as much as possible.
